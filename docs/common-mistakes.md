# Common mistakes — the tripwires, in one place

These are the mistakes almost everyone makes in their first month. Not because you're doing anything wrong — because nobody shows you the *first* lesson in this topic, and everyone hits the same three walls in the same order.

We collect them here, kindly, with the 10-second fix for each.

## The 10 mistakes (and the 10-second fix)

1. **Being vague, then blaming the tool.**
   *Fix:* before pressing enter, ask yourself: did I say who this is for, what should be in it, and what it should feel like?

2. **Publishing the first draft.**
   *Fix:* always ask a round two: "make it shorter / warmer / like a human would text." Draft one is never the whole product.

3. **Trusting it about anything serious.**
   *Fix:* for anything individual and important — doses, dates, money, legal language, someone's health — verify it with a real source: the official site, the pharmacist, your accountant. Asking the same tool "are you sure?" isn't a check.

4. **Believing its citations without looking.**
   *Fix:* click two sources from anything you'd want a court, a boss, or a family to rely on. Two clicks. That's the test.

5. **Reading a tutorial when you could have tried it.**
   *Fix:* this is the most common mistake in the entire genre. Open the tool *now* and do today's step. This manual will happily wait.

6. **Pasting in sensitive stuff.**
   *Fix:* describe it instead of pasting it ("a letter about a mold repair issue"), and never share passwords, codes, or anyone else's private information. Take it up with the coffee-shop stranger.

7. **Not saying "no."**
   *Fix:* you get to give directives. "Remove that last paragraph." "I don't like this language." "Redo it for a busy single mom audience." Pushing back is the skill; learning it will save you thousands of hours.

8. **Assuming every tool needs an "aha moment."**
   *Fix:* if a tool doesn't feel natural after a week of light use, it was the wrong tool for you. Drop it without ceremony. There are a hundred more.

9. **Asking for a masterpiece on the first try and getting mush.**
   *Fix:* start smaller. "List 5 ideas, each one sentence." Then ask it to expand the good one. Momentum beats genius.

10. **Letting perfectionism postpone being useful.**
    *Fix:* the bar is *is this useful*, not *is this magical*. Ten minutes with an AI tool beats zero minutes waiting for the perfect stack, plan, and podcast on the subject.

## The pattern under all ten

Every single one of these is a version of one thing:

> **The tool is easy. Knowing what you want is the work.**

Which is a relief, honestly — because "setting goals" means real life experience that you already have. You walk in with something the average machine lacks: a life. Keep it that way: your experience in, AI's speed out. Nobody gets to gatekeep you for that.

## If you only remember one thing

Do the "Try it now" step. Any of them. Today. Ten minutes ago is ideal; right now is fine.
