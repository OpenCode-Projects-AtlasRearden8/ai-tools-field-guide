# Glossary — every term, in plain English

Every word in this book that an expert might use as a shibboleth, defined like we're talking in your kitchen. If a term isn't here, it didn't need to be in the book — that was a choice, not an accident.

## The basics

**AI (artificial intelligence)** — An umbrella word for computer systems that do things which seem clever. Most of what you'll touch is the second thing below, not the doomsday-movie thing.

**Model** — The "brain" inside an AI tool. It's a giant mathematical pattern-cruncher trained on enormous amounts of data. You don't need to know anything else about it, promise.

**Chat assistant (or chatbot)** — A tool you talk to in ordinary language and it replies. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot — four logos for the same basic experience.

**Prompt** — Whatever you type into a chat assistant. A good prompt says what you want, for whom, and in what style. Not a magic spell. Planning out loud.

## The words based on "learning" or "training"

**Training** — The process where a model studies millions of examples and ends up with patterns it can use. Trained-on-data is where a tool's voice knowledge and preferences come from.

**Hallucination (the A.I. word for "it misstated something")** — When an AI tool says something wrong *with total confidence*. Not a glitch, a nature feature. It is a guessing machine that guesses well.

## The tool-by-tool words

**Transcription (speech-to-text)** — Speech turned into text. Your phone's microphone-on-the-keyboard does this; so do the meeting apps.

**Text-to-speech (TTS)** — Text turned into speech. The "voices" reading articles and directions.

**Image generation** — Type a description, get a picture. The pictures are the model's guess at your brief.

**Editing / photo cleanup** — Same tools pointed at photos you upload: remove the background, brighten, denoise, resize. The most practically useful half of image AI, and everybody forgets it exists.

**AI search** — Search that reads its sources and reports back (Perplexity, ChatGPT's Search, Gemini). Sources are listed so *you* can do the final check.

## The "how it works" words, mostly for reading the news

**LLM (large language model)** — The widespread name for the kind of model behind chat assistants. "Language" means it's trained on text, so it writes better than it does most everything else.

**Tokens** — The little chunks of text models actually read and produce (roughly words). You only need this word for the pricing page on some tools ("1M tokens free"). Concept, not homework.

**Fine-tuning** — Training a model a bit more on a narrower subject so it's extra good at that niche. From your seat: a "specialty model."

**API** — The pipes that let two pieces of software talk. Relevant only if you build apps. You'll never need it to *use* anything in this book.

**Open source** — Software whose insides are public. There are free "open-source models" you can run on your own computer with no monthly bill, no data going anywhere — that's the "local AI" world, covered in *The Massive AI Manual*.

## The "what can go wrong" words

**Hallucination** — (again, because it matters) it misstates things with a confident voice. The check: real sources for anything serious.

**Bias** — The tool inherits loose patterns from its training data, including the stereotypes in it. Relevant mostly when it's making *decisions* about people — like hiring — which is exactly why you keep humans in that loop.

**Privacy / "your data"** — The what-you-type-in question. The coffee-shop-stranger rule from [Everyday safety](everyday-safety.html) covers the beginner side of it completely.

## Words you'll see in pricing plans

**Free tier** — A free version with limits (usually daily-use caps and fewer features). Every tool in this book has one honest one.

**Pro / Plus / Premium** — The paid version. For chat assistants, worth it only if you're using it daily for something you care about. There is no reason to pay on day one.

## Words that sound impressive and secretly aren't

**Agentic AI / agents** — AI that *does a task* rather than just chats: books the flight, not just recommends one. Emerging, over-hyped, and still worth knowing the name for. (*The Massive AI Manual* has a whole chapter.)

**"Sentient" / "alive" / "it knows"** — Marketing words for what is still a very fast, very well-read, well-mannered guessing machine. It doesn't feel anything about your day. It's doing an incredible imitation of feeling something about your day.
