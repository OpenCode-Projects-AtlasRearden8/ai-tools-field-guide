# Search tools: finding better answers, faster

A *search tool* is the AI-flavored version of the thing you already do: asking the internet questions. Instead of giving you ten blue links and hoping one helps, an **AI-powered search tool** (like ChatGPT with *Search*, Perplexity, or Gemini) reads several sources, pulls out what matters, and hands you a tidy answer — often *with the sources listed* so you can verify it, which is the part people forget to be glad about.

## What it actually does

A normal search box matches your words to web pages (that's why typos and weird phrasing break it badly). An AI search tool does two passes: it searches, then it *reads what it found* and reports back. That second pass is where the magic is — and also where the risk lives, because its reading is still the same kind of educated guess you've learned about. The tool gets it wrong most often when it strays outside its sources; the fix is to notice when it supports itself.

## Try it now (3 minutes, free)

Use **ChatGPT with the Search button**, or **Perplexity** (perplexity.ai), or **Google's "AI Mode"** in search. Try the same question three ways and watch what changes:

1. **The plain question** (the way you'd type it into Google):

   > how long does a flight from chicago to berlin take

2. **The researched question** (add *what you already know* and *what's peculiar about your case*):

   > I'm flying Chicago to Berlin in January with one checked bag and a spray bottle of saline in my carry-on. How long is the flight, and is the saline okay as carry-on per TSA rules now? Cite the TSA page if you can.

   What changed: you gave context (January — snow delays; saline — medication rules), and you asked it to *show its receipts*. When a tool cites sources, you, the human, are the final check. That partnership is the whole game.

3. **The follow-up** (the thing old search never let you do politely):

   > Now: what should I book for dinner near Hauptbahnhof that fits a $12 budget and doesn't require reservations?

   Notice the search tool didn't flinch. It treats your parentheticals and follow-up questions as normal human speech — because to it, they are.

## When to use AI search vs. plain search

| Situation | Reach for |
| --- | --- |
| Quick fact ("what's the capital of Peru?") | Either works; AI search saves a grade-school level of link clicking |
| "Best of" or comparisons ("best budget espresso machine 2026") | AI search — it reads the round-up articles and reviews for you |
| Anything medical, legal, or financial that's personal and serious | AI search for orientation, then the official source (in this case: your state board, the IRS, your doctor) |
| Anything involving your own accounts or employer's portal | Plain search — follow the official page; a summarized answer can't log in for you |

## The reflex worth stealing

**"How do you know?"** — asked calmly, this one question upgrades every AI answer you get.

Good answer: lists sources ("per the TSA page…, per United's route page…").
Bad answer: sounds authoritative and cites nothing.

Unsourced ≠ wrong. Sourced ≠ right. But *sourced answers are checkable*, and checkable beats confident every single time.

## Where beginners bash their knees on the coffee table

- **Asking for a recommendation and imagining the years of expertise.** Sizes, stock levels, and "best of" listicles can be stale — the tool reads them as written, old broken links and all. For purchases, check the "page last updated" dates it shows; it's happier when you mention a timeframe.
- **Trust it with a needle, not a scalpel.** Perfect for "what's this word mean" and "how does a mortgage rate work." Not the tool to interpret your specific mortgage document — but a *great* tool for "what words in here should I Google?"

> Mantra for the week: *read the sources, not just the answer.*
